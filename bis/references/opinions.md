# Opinions & Hot Takes — BIS

> Use one strong opinion per blog post, max. These come from BIS's actual positioning work, not invented takes — deploy them, don't dilute them.

## On the industry

- **A named contact beats a bigger company, every time a business actually needs help.** BIS isn't trying to out-scale AliSom/TechnoMobi on paper — it can't, and pretending otherwise would be the least BIS thing to do. The lever size makes structurally hard to pull for a bigger franchisee is one person who already knows your account. That's the whole pitch, said plainly instead of dressed up as "personalised service."
- **"Book a consultation" is a red flag phrase.** It signals a sales process standing between a business and an actual answer. BIS's own standing rule is "request a callback" — smaller step, faster answer, no pitch deck required first.
- **A provider that can't give you a response time in hours, in writing, is telling you something.** Vague SLAs ("prompt support," "priority response") usually mean the number would be embarrassing if they wrote it down.

## On buying decisions

- **Loss framing beats savings framing, and BIS should say so out loud.** Most vendors lead with what a business gains. BIS's best-performing content leads with what breaks first — downtime, missed calls, a stalled POS terminal — because that's what actually moves a decision, and pretending otherwise is just worse marketing.
- **The champion inside the business is doing unpaid sales work for you.** Every proposal, one-pager, and even a stray blog post might get forwarded cold to someone who's never heard of BIS. If it doesn't hold up out of context, it's not finished.
- **Switching providers only feels risky because most providers make it feel risky on purpose.** Long contracts, vague exit terms, and a handover process nobody explains upfront. Plain terms and a named contact are supposed to make the first step small — that's a design choice, not an accident.

## On honesty as a competitive edge

- **Refusing to publish a fake case study is a brand asset, not a gap to paper over.** Plenty of competitors will show a glossy "results" page with numbers nobody can verify. BIS saying "case studies are landing once the audit's done" costs a little polish today and buys a lot of trust with the exact buyer (procurement, IT) who's trained to be suspicious of unverifiable claims.

## How to use these

- One opinion per post, backed by something concrete from `voice.md` or the real numbers in `stats.md` once populated.
- Never invent a new hot take that isn't grounded in BIS's actual positioning — these five come from the brand work already done; don't drift from them.
