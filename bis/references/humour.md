# Humour: Business Intelligent Solutions (BIS)

> Read this alongside `voice.md`, `opinions.md` and `stories.md` before writing any BIS blog post.
> Created 06/10/2026. BIS humour is dry, specific and rare. If a line needs explaining, it goes.

## The north star

Picture the senior IT manager who has sat through every outage, every vendor pitch and every "quick question" at 16:55 on a Friday. In the meeting, they say the one dry line everyone was thinking. Then they get back to fixing the problem.

That's the register: a raised eyebrow, never a stand-up set. The reader should half-smile and keep reading, because the next sentence is useful.

BIS and vShopper sound different on purpose. vShopper is the friend in the group chat with a pun ready. BIS is the colleague whose understatement you quote later. No exclamation marks, no puns in headings, no "fun brand" energy.

## Where humour is allowed

| Format | Humour? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Blog posts (`/insights`) | Yes | Body copy only. See placement rules below. |
| Educational social posts (carousels, short posts, polls) | Light | One line at most, usually in the hook. |
| Deal and promotional posts | No | Price, specs and a callback. Nothing else. |
| Service pages, proposals, sales collateral | No | A procurement buyer reads these as claims. |
| CTAs ("Request a callback") | No | The CTA stays plain and clear every time. |
| Titles, excerpts, meta descriptions, FAQ answers | No | These are for Google and for skimmers. Clarity wins. |

## Six rules

1. **Understatement beats jokes.** Say less than the situation deserves and let the reader fill in the rest. "A digger and a fibre duct in the same street rarely end well for the duct" works because it's calm about something infuriating.

2. **Punch at the situation, the habit or ourselves.** Fair targets are cable theft (the situation), vague contracts, hold music, the shared drive nobody tidies, the router living in the stationery cupboard, and the IT industry's love of acronyms. Never aim at the reader, their staff, a named competitor or anyone who has been a victim of crime or fraud.

3. **The laugh comes from a real detail.** Generic jokes about "technology being hard" are filler. Specific ones land: the folder called "FINAL FINAL v3", the asterisk next to "unlimited", the backup SIM that got suspended for not being used. If the detail isn't true to how South African businesses actually work, cut the line.

4. **One line, then back to work.** A humour moment is one sentence or one bracketed aside. Never a paragraph, never a running gag, and it never replaces the useful point it sits next to.

5. **Local and evergreen.** Use South African references that will still read fine in two years:
   - the load-shedding years (past tense)
   - municipal roadworks
   - the bakkie
   - "now now"
   - Highveld storms taking out a tower
   - the braai where someone asks you to fix their Wi-Fi

   Avoid memes, current-events jokes and anything that dates in a month.

6. **Brackets are free real estate.** A short aside lets the joke sit to one side of the sentence: "(yes, the cupboard with the router in it counts as your server room)". It keeps the main sentence clean for the skimmer.

## How often

- One moment roughly every 450 to 500 words.
- A 2,200 to 2,800 word post lands 4 to 6 moments, no more.
- One of them can be the last line of the closing section, just above the CTA, never inside it.
- If a post is about a loss someone suffered (fraud, a breach, a stolen phone), use 2 to 3 moments at most, all aimed at the attacker's tactics or the process gap, never at the person.

## Patterns that work (with BIS examples)

Write fresh lines each time. These show the shape and are not meant to be pasted.

**The calm understatement**
- "Two fibre lines through the same duct are, in practical terms, one fibre line with two invoices."
- "Most cloud migrations go well until someone remembers the accounting software from 2014."

**The honest admission**
- "Nobody opens a blog post hoping to read about failover rules. Stay for the next section anyway; it's the one that saves a Saturday."
- "Yes, we sell connectivity. We'd still rather you read the contract before signing it."

**The recognisable detail**
- "Somewhere on your shared drive there's a folder called 'NEW FOLDER (2)'. It contains the only copy of something important."
- "The backup SIM has been in the router drawer since 2023. Its data bundle expired around the same time as its welcome SMS."

**The bracketed aside**
- "Test the failover every quarter (on a Tuesday morning, preferably, not ten minutes before month-end payroll)."
- "Ask for the response time in hours, in writing (a provider who suddenly needs to 'check with the team' has answered you)."

**The dry close**
- "Set the reminder for month 34. Future you will be pleasantly unbothered."
- "Fix it before the next outage asks the question for you."

## By solution page: angles that suit each topic

| Solution | Fair game | Leave alone |
|---|---|---|
| Business Internet | diggers, wayleaves, the router in the cupboard, "unlimited" fine print, Highveld storms | blaming a specific fibre network operator |
| Mobile & Voice | hold music, "your call is important to us", desk phones nobody has unplugged since 2015, the personal cellphone that became the main line | jokes about accents or call-centre staff |
| Cloud & Hosting | the server under the reception desk, "the cloud is just someone else's computer" (once, max), migration surprises | POPIA penalties, compliance failures as jokes |
| Modern Workplace | unused Microsoft 365 licences, version-control chaos, "reply all", 47 Teams channels | mocking staff who aren't tech-savvy |
| Security | attacker tactics that are obvious in hindsight, urgent Friday payment requests, password-on-a-sticky-note | victims of fraud, theft or hijacking; actual losses |
| IoT | spreadsheets updated "daily" (weekly), "where's the bakkie?" phone calls | tracking staff in a way that sounds like surveillance |
| SD-WAN / multi-branch | the fourth-branch problem, every store running a different router "for historical reasons" | naming real retailers or chains |
| Contracts and costs | month-36 billing, auto-renewals, exit terms in 6pt font | jokes about anyone's finances or budget |

## Hard no

- Politics, politicians, government departments, race, language, accents, religion, gender, age.
- Crime as a punchline: cable theft, hijacking, smash-and-grab or fraud are situations to plan around, never jokes. Dry lines about the process ("the battery thieves don't wait for your maintenance window") are the limit.
- Load-shedding as a current-tense gag. Refer to it as history or as a risk to plan for.
- Named competitors, named clients or named real businesses.
- Anything that makes a reader feel stupid for not knowing a technical term.
- Exclamation marks and emojis.
- Puns in titles, H2s or excerpts. A mild pun in body copy, once per post, maximum.
- AI-style filler humour: "Spoiler alert:", "Plot twist:", "Let's be honest", "We get it, IT is boring", "Buckle up", "Here's the kicker".
- Sarcasm aimed at the customer's choices, setup or spend.
- Contrastive "it's not X, it's Y" punchlines and any banned word or structure from `voice.md` and Shany's writing standard.

## Before and after

**Too much**
> Ah, fibre outages! Every business owner's favourite surprise! Grab a coffee, because you'll be on hold for a while!

**Too little**
> Fibre outages can disrupt business operations and cause downtime.

**BIS**
> A contractor's digger finds your fibre duct at 9am. By 9:01 the card machines are offline and someone is holding a phone to their ear, listening to hold music they'll know by heart before lunch.

## Vibe check before publishing

Read each humour line out loud and ask:

- Would the IT manager everyone trusts say this in a meeting? ✓
- Does it still make sense to someone forwarded the post cold, with no context? ✓
- Does it rest on a real, specific detail? ✓
- Could anyone reading feel laughed at? ✗ If yes, cut it.
- Would a procurement buyer read it as BIS not taking the problem seriously? ✗ If yes, cut it.
- Does it sound like a brand account trying to be relatable? ✗ If yes, cut it.

When in doubt, leave it out. A useful post with no jokes is fine. A post with one bad joke is worse than that.
