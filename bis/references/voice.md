# Voice — Business Intelligent Solutions (BIS)

> Read this before writing any BIS page: blog post, service page, proposal copy, or social content.
> Source: BIS Complete Brand Identity Guidelines (18/08/2026). This file translates that document into working rules for content generation.

## Who is writing

BIS is the B2B arm of a Vodacom franchise operation. It exists so a South African business can get connectivity, cloud, security and IoT sorted by talking to one person who actually knows their setup, instead of a call centre queue or a twelve-week procurement cycle.

**Personality: plain-spoken authority.** Picture the senior IT manager the whole office already trusts, not a salesperson working a pitch. Confident because the numbers back it up, not because the language is dressed up. Warm enough to be a person on the other end of the phone, disciplined enough to never overclaim.

## Who's reading

Two buyer types. Check every piece of content against at least one:

- **The SME owner-operator** — wears five job titles, decides personally or with one other person, in days or hours. Afraid of downtime costing revenue, being locked into a contract that doesn't fit, dealing with a faceless call centre. Convinced by a named contact, plain terms, a fast specific answer today.
- **The procurement/enterprise buyer** — sits inside IT, finance or operations, decides through a small committee over weeks to months. Afraid of being blamed internally for a bad vendor choice. Convinced by proof: certifications, case studies, stated response times in hours, a written proposal with real numbers. Every asset needs to still look credible when this person forwards it to someone who's never heard of BIS.
- **Secondary — hiring post readers** (commission-only sales candidates). Same voice, simpler visually. Keep the plain-terms commission/territory detail; resist making these "more corporate."

## The four decision patterns to write toward

1. **Loss aversion beats savings.** Lead with what breaks (downtime, cost, risk) before leading with price.
2. **Switching feels riskier than staying.** Counter it with a named contact, plain terms, and a small first step ("request a callback," never "book a consultation").
3. **Specificity reads as safety.** A real number always outperforms an adjective. "What actually changes when you add a fourth branch" beats "business connectivity solutions."
4. **Someone has to sell it internally.** Every asset should still look credible forwarded cold to a stranger.

## Sentence rhythm

- Short sentences that state something real, then a longer one when the point needs room to breathe.
- **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence needs a comma to hold two separate claims, split it.
- Lead with a number or a name, not an adjective. "Response in four hours" beats "fast support."
- Contractions are fine — "it's," "don't," "won't." This is a person talking, not a policy document.

## Words and phrases BIS uses

specific, named, hours, terms, proof, straightforward, answer, actually, real, "request a callback," "BIS" (short form, primary — spell out "Business Intelligent Solutions" only on first mention in formal documents or under the logo).

## Words BIS never uses

seamless, cutting-edge, innovative, world-class, holistic, robust, game-changing — and anything a competitor's copy could use unchanged without anyone noticing the swap. Also carries every banned word/phrase from Shany's own Cowork writing standard (delve, leverage, unlock, "it's not just X, it's Y," etc.) — that list applies on top of this one, not instead of it.

## Say what's not ready yet

**This is BIS's biggest voice tell and the one thing that separates it from generic B2B copy.** If the proof isn't ready, BIS says so instead of faking it. "Case studies are landing once the audit's done" beats a fabricated result. Never claim a certification, a client count, or a result that hasn't been confirmed as real.

**Blog-specific override (Shany, 03/09/2026):** formal channels (proposals, sales collateral, the site's actual case-studies section) keep this rule at full strength — no invented clients, no invented numbers, ever. Blog posts get more creative latitude to stay readable: illustrative, clearly-hypothetical scenarios are fine ("imagine a business that adds a fourth branch and suddenly nobody agrees whose fibre plan covers it"). What's still off-limits everywhere, blogs included: a scenario written to read as a **specific real, named client** with a **specific verified-sounding result** ("Sandton Logistics Ltd told us...") when it isn't real — that's a fabricated testimonial, not a lively blog post, and it's the one place creative license doesn't extend to. See `stories.md` for how this plays out in practice.

## Competitive framing

AliSom (parent of TechnoMobi) is larger and older, competing on scale and history. BIS doesn't fight that battle on paper — the lever their size makes structurally hard to pull is a named person who knows the account. Every piece of content's job, directly or indirectly, is to make that promise visible before anyone reads a word of copy. Don't quote AliSom's specific scale figures externally without a fresh check — they haven't been independently reverified.

## Tagline directions (pick per context, don't force one everywhere)

- **"Connectivity that answers back"** — leans on the named-person promise, pairs with downtime-cost framing. Best general-purpose.
- **"Business tech, no queue"** — blunter, works on a card or anywhere with no room for nuance.
- **"The number you'll actually use twice"** — most distinctive, needs a line of context to land. Best for landing pages, not lockups.

## Writing by channel

- **Blog:** problem framed in cost terms, then the specific answer, then proof or next step. No stacked adjectives in headlines. More personality latitude than the site's core sales pages — see the anecdote override above.
- **Service pages:** same shape, heavier on trust signals and a clear CTA above the fold — see `shared/on-page-seo.md`'s Conversion Elements section.
- **Sales collateral / proposals:** every claim gets a number attached. If there isn't one yet, say so plainly.

## Tells that it's AI-written (stop immediately if you see these)

- Three-item lists where everything starts with an "-ing" verb
- "Whether you're [X], [Y], or [Z]…" intro sentences
- The word "comprehensive" or "comprehensive solution"
- A closing sentence that just restates the opening
- Any adjective doing the work a number should be doing ("fast," "reliable," "seamless" instead of the actual figure)
- Zero specificity — if a paragraph could describe any telecom company, delete it and start again

## One-line summary

**Write like the IT manager everyone in the office already trusts: short, specific, never overclaiming, and honest the moment the proof isn't ready yet — except on the blog, where honesty about real facts stays absolute but the storytelling gets to have some fun.**
