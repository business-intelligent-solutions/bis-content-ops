# Humour — vShopper

> Every vShopper blog post should have personality. This file is how — read it alongside `voice.md`.

## The north star

The friend in the group chat who actually knows phones, gets asked "which one should I buy" constantly, and answers with a joke first and then the real advice. Not a comedian. Not a corporate account trying to be "relatable" and missing.

## Five rules

1. **Puns and dad jokes on the topic are welcome, not mandatory in every paragraph.** A blocked-drain-style pun about "buffering" or "low battery energy" lands when it's earned, not forced into every section.
2. **Punch at the situation, never at the reader.** The person reading has a cracked screen or a confusing contract, not a personal failing. Jokes about load shedding, data prices, the industry, or vShopper's own quirks are fair game. Jokes at the customer's expense are not.
3. **South African cultural references, used sparingly and kept evergreen.** Load shedding, taxi WhatsApp groups, the group chat that never dies, braai small talk. Avoid anything that dates fast (a meme that's dead in a month) — this needs to still read fine a year from now.
4. **Parenthetical asides are free real estate.** "(yes, still on 3G in some spots — we know)" — small, human, doesn't derail the sentence.
5. **The sign-off should land, not just wrap up.** Not "Shop now for the best deals!" — something with a bit of personality that still points at the actual next step.

## Anti-patterns

- Exclamation marks — the joke should land deadpan, not need punctuation to sell it
- Emojis
- Outdated meme references
- Forced quirk — if a joke isn't actually funny, cut it rather than leave a weak one in
- Making fun of the customer's situation (old phone, tight budget, confused about contracts) — that's the opposite of the "friend who helps" voice

## Frequency

Roughly one moment — a joke, a pun, a self-aware aside — every 300–400 words. A 1,000–1,200 word post should land 3–4 of these plus one sign-off that makes the reader smile.

## Vibe check before shipping

Does it sound like:
✓ Someone who actually works in mobile retail, texting a friend who asked for advice
✗ A brand account trying too hard to be relatable
✗ A stand-up set
✗ Generic AI cheerfulness with no specific jokes at all
