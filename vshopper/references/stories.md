## Illustrative scenarios (fair game for blog posts)

Use these to make a point concrete and funny, not to fake social proof.

- **Never write a fake customer quote or review presented as real.** "One customer told us..." attached to an invented quote reads as a fabricated testimonial, not a joke — that's off-limits regardless of how obviously exaggerated it is, because it's still presented as something a real person said.
- **Generic, unnamed, clearly-a-device scenarios are fine.** "Picture the group chat friend who's been on the same cracked screen since 2019" is a relatable bit, not a claim about a real person.
- **Keep it light and specific to the situation, not to a fabricated outcome.** A joke about load shedding killing a router mid-Zoom call is fine. "This fixed 200 customers' downtime" is not — that's an invented statistic wearing a joke's clothing.

### Starter scenarios (adapt, don't recite verbatim)

- **The contract-renewal spiral.** Someone gets the renewal SMS, opens five tabs comparing deals, and closes all of them out of decision fatigue. Use for SIM-only vs. contract posts, upgrade-timing posts.
- **The "it still works, technically" phone.** A phone held together by a case, a prayer, and a screen protector doing the screen's job for it. Use for trade-in, repair-vs-replace, or new-release posts.
- **The load-shedding fibre panic.** Everything's fine until stage 4 hits mid-download. Use for connectivity/business-fibre posts, backup power + router posts.

## How to use this file

- Real stories: only once verified and supplied by Shany.
